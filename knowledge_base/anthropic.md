# Anthropic

## Overview

Anthropic (legally Anthropic, PBC) is an American artificial intelligence company that develops the Claude family of large language models and the products built on them, including the Claude apps, the Claude Developer Platform (API), Claude Code and Claude Cowork. Anthropic is headquartered at 500 Howard Street, San Francisco, California. Anthropic is organised as a public benefit corporation whose stated mission centres on AI safety, and it describes itself as a safety-focused frontier lab. As of September 2026 Anthropic is privately held, valued at $865 billion after its May 2026 Series H round, and has confidentially filed for an initial public offering (IPO).

## Founding and leadership

Anthropic was founded in 2021 (Wikipedia gives 26 January 2021) by a group of former OpenAI staff: siblings Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei, Jared Kaplan, Jack Clark, Chris Olah, Ben Mann, Sam McCandlish and Tom Brown. Dario Amodei is Anthropic's chief executive officer and Daniela Amodei is Anthropic's president. Other senior Anthropic leaders include Jared Kaplan (chief science officer), Mike Krieger (chief product officer, formerly Instagram co-founder) and Krishna Rao (chief financial officer). Anthropic's headcount is not officially disclosed and estimates vary widely: third-party trackers cited in April 2026 ranged from about 2,500 (PitchBook) to about 3,000 (TrueUp) to about 5,000 (Tracxn). Figures for Anthropic employees should therefore be treated as estimates.

## Funding, ownership and valuation

Anthropic's funding history, in brief:

- **2021–2022:** early rounds of $124 million (May 2021) and $580 million (April 2022, including $500 million from FTX).
- **2023–2024:** Amazon committed a total of $8 billion (tranches in September 2023, March 2024 and November 2024); Google invested roughly $2.5 billion across several tranches from October 2023, plus a further $1 billion in 2025.
- **Series E (March 2025):** $3.5 billion at a $61.5 billion post-money valuation.
- **Series F (2 September 2025):** $13 billion at a $183 billion post-money valuation, led by ICONIQ with Fidelity and Lightspeed co-leading. Anthropic reported run-rate revenue rising from about $1 billion in early 2025 to over $5 billion by August 2025, and over 300,000 business customers.
- **Series G (12 February 2026):** $30 billion at a $380 billion post-money valuation, led by GIC and Coatue, with Microsoft and Nvidia participating. Crunchbase called it the second-largest venture deal ever. Anthropic reported run-rate revenue of $14 billion, more than 500 customers spending over $1 million a year, and 8 of the Fortune 10 as customers.
- **Series H (28 May 2026):** $65 billion at a $865 billion post-money valuation, led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia. It included $15 billion of hyperscaler commitments ($5 billion from Amazon). Anthropic's run-rate revenue had crossed $47 billion earlier in May 2026.

Anthropic has raised roughly $130 billion in total (press estimate). In August 2026 Bloomberg and TechCrunch reported that Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate had passed $65 billion as of late July 2026, up from about $9 billion at the end of 2025. Investors expect Anthropic to finish 2026 with $100–120 billion in revenue; these are reported projections, not company guidance. On 1 June 2026 Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft Form S-1 to the US SEC. The press reported a target listing valuation of $2 trillion or more. Reports in late September 2026 said the IPO had slipped from October to November 2026 (reported, not confirmed by Anthropic).

## Products and distribution

Anthropic sells Claude through consumer and team subscriptions (Free, Pro at $20 per month, Max at $100–$200 per month, Team and Enterprise) and through pay-per-token API access on the Claude Developer Platform. Claude models are also distributed through Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud Vertex AI and Microsoft Azure/Foundry. Anthropic's agentic products include Claude Code (a terminal, IDE and web coding agent), Claude Cowork (desktop and web, launched January 2026), Claude for Chrome (August 2025) and Claude Design (April 2026). Claude Code's run-rate revenue exceeded $2.5 billion by February 2026, and research firm SemiAnalysis estimated (February 2026) that about 4% of public GitHub commits were authored by Claude Code. Anthropic does not publish consumer user totals. TechCrunch (March 2026) cited estimates of 18–30 million consumer users, and Anthropic said paid subscriptions had more than doubled in early 2026. Enterprise revenue dominates: Anthropic had over 300,000 business customers as of 2025, and estimates put 70–75% of revenue from API usage. Compute deals announced alongside the Series H include up to 5 GW with Amazon, 5 GW of TPU capacity with Google and Broadcom, and GPU access at SpaceX/xAI's Colossus clusters.

## Research, safety and openness

Anthropic does not release open-weights frontier models. Claude is available only through Anthropic's hosted products and cloud partners. Anthropic's safety governance centres on its Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) and AI Safety Levels (ASL). RSP version 3.0, effective 24 February 2026, added a public Frontier Safety Roadmap and quarterly Risk Reports. RSP v3.0 also dropped the earlier implied commitment to pause development, which drew criticism from some safety commentators. Anthropic pioneered Constitutional AI; Claude's constitution grew from about 2,700 words in 2023 to about 23,000 words in 2026. Anthropic also publishes interpretability research and system cards for each model.

In April 2026 Anthropic withheld Claude Mythos from public release because of its cybersecurity capabilities. Instead, Anthropic shared Mythos with critical-infrastructure partners through Project Glasswing. On 12 June 2026 a US government directive forced Anthropic to suspend all access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5. Mythos 5 was restored to US organisations after government approval on 26 June, and Fable 5 was redeployed globally on 1 July 2026.

Anthropic is also in a dispute with the US Department of Defense. The dispute began after Anthropic refused to drop contract limits on use for mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. The Pentagon designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk in February 2026. On 28 August 2026 Judge Rita Lin of a federal district court in California ruled that designation unlawful. On 25 September 2026, however, the DC Circuit Court of Appeals upheld a parallel designation by a 2–1 vote. In September 2025 Anthropic agreed a $1.5 billion settlement with authors (Bartz v. Anthropic) over pirated books used in training, about $3,000 per book.
