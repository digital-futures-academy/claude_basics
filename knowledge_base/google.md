# Google

## Overview
Google is the main subsidiary of Alphabet Inc., a US public company (Nasdaq: GOOGL, GOOG) headquartered in Mountain View, California. Google's AI work is led by Google DeepMind, the London-headquartered research lab that builds the Gemini family of proprietary models and the Gemma family of open-weight models. Google is vertically integrated in AI: it designs its own Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), runs Google Cloud (including Vertex AI and Gemini Enterprise), and distributes AI to billions of users through the Gemini app, Search (AI Overviews and AI Mode), Android and Workspace. This profile covers Google as an AI organisation rather than its advertising business.

## Founding and leadership
Google was founded in 1998 by Larry Page and Sergey Brin; Alphabet became its holding company in 2015. Sundar Pichai is CEO of both Google and Alphabet, and Anat Ashkenazi is Alphabet's CFO. DeepMind was founded in London in 2010 by Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg and Mustafa Suleyman and acquired by Google in 2014; it merged with Google Brain to form Google DeepMind in April 2023. On 5 August 2026 Google announced a leadership change: Demis Hassabis stepped back from day-to-day operations to become Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet (and continues to lead Isomorphic Labs), while Koray Kavukcuoglu, previously Google DeepMind CTO and Google's Chief AI Architect, became Senior Vice President leading Google DeepMind, reporting to Pichai and overseeing Gemini models, frontier research and the Gemini app and developer teams. Some press describe Kavukcuoglu as DeepMind's new "CEO", but Google's announcement uses the SVP title. Jeff Dean, Google's long-time chief scientist, announced the same day that he is leaving after 27 years to found an independent public benefit corporation with Sanjay Ghemawat, with Google as a founding investor. Alphabet had 198,933 employees at 30 June 2026.

## Funding, ownership and valuation
As a public company, Google's AI investment is best measured through capital expenditure, cloud revenue and strategic stakes.
- **Capex:** In February 2026 Alphabet guided to $175–185 billion of 2026 capex; it raised this to $180–190 billion in April 2026 and to $195–205 billion on 22 July 2026. Q2 2026 capex was $44.9 billion (about double the $22.4 billion a year earlier), with about 60% going to servers (TPUs and GPUs). Management said 2027 capex will increase significantly.
- **Revenue:** Alphabet's annual revenue exceeded $400 billion for the first time in 2025. In Q2 2026 Alphabet revenue was $119.8 billion (+24%), and Google Cloud revenue was $24.8 billion (+82%) with $8.8 billion operating income and a cloud backlog of about $514 billion (up from $240 billion at end-2025). Q2 2026 net income of $112.1 billion was inflated by roughly $98 billion of unrealised gains on equity holdings such as Anthropic and SpaceX; free cash flow was negative (about –$5.9 billion) for the quarter.
- **Anthropic:** Google is a long-standing Anthropic investor and TPU supplier. On 24 April 2026 Google agreed to invest up to $40 billion in Anthropic: $10 billion immediately at a $350 billion valuation plus up to $30 billion tied to milestones, together with 5 gigawatts of Google Cloud capacity over five years. This followed an earlier deal for up to one million TPUs and a Google–Broadcom arrangement for 3.5 gigawatts of TPU capacity from 2027.

## Products and distribution
Google distributes AI through consumer, developer and enterprise channels. The **Gemini app** had over 750 million monthly active users (MAU) in Q4 2025, 900 million at Google I/O in May 2026, and 850 million in Q2 2026 (reported 22 July 2026). In Search, **AI Overviews** has about 2.5 billion MAU and **AI Mode** passed 1 billion MAU in Q2 2026. Developers use the **Gemini API**, Google AI Studio, Gemini CLI and the Antigravity agentic IDE; Google said more than 9 million developers build with its models monthly (Q2 2026) and that its APIs processed about 22 billion tokens per minute in Q2 2026 (up from 10 billion per minute in Q4 2025). Across all surfaces Google processed more than 3.2 quadrillion tokens per month by May 2026. Enterprises use **Vertex AI** and **Gemini Enterprise**, which had more than 8 million paid seats by early 2026 and is used by nearly 90% of the Fortune 100. Consumer subscriptions include Google AI Pro and Google AI Ultra; Alphabet reported more than 325 million paid subscriptions across services at end-2025. TPUs are sold through Google Cloud to customers including Anthropic.

## Research, safety and openness
Google follows a dual approach to openness: Gemini models are proprietary and API-only, while Gemma models are released as open weights. Gemma 4 (April 2026) moved to the permissive Apache 2.0 licence, and at launch the Gemma family had passed 400 million downloads and more than 100,000 community variants; by the Q2 2026 earnings call Sundar Pichai put Gemma downloads at over 900 million. Google DeepMind's **Frontier Safety Framework** (first published May 2024) defines Critical Capability Levels for CBRN, cyber, harmful manipulation, and machine-learning R&D/misalignment risks; version 3.0 came in September 2025 and version 3.1 on 17 April 2026, adding lower "Tracked Capability Levels" and a stronger "Security Level 2+" against insider threats. Google publishes FSF reports for frontier models (for example Gemini 3 Pro, November 2025) and watermarks AI content with SynthID (over 100 billion images and videos marked). Notable research includes the Transformer (2017), AlphaGo, AlphaFold (2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for Hassabis and John Jumper) and Gemini Robotics.
