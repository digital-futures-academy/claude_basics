# Meta

## Overview
Meta Platforms, Inc. is a publicly listed US technology company (Nasdaq: META) headquartered in Menlo Park, California, best known for Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger. As an AI organisation, Meta runs one of the largest AI research and infrastructure operations in the world, now consolidated under Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL), which includes the long-standing FAIR research group. Meta's AI work powers the Meta AI assistant across its apps, Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses and a developer API. Meta was historically the leading champion of open-weight models through the Llama family (see the separate Llama profile), but in 2026 its flagship models are the proprietary Muse series, alongside selected open-weight releases.

## Founding and leadership
Meta was founded as Facebook on 4 February 2004 by Mark Zuckerberg and co-founders, and renamed Meta Platforms in October 2021. Mark Zuckerberg remains Meta's chairman and CEO. Meta's AI research began with Facebook AI Research (FAIR), founded in 2013 with Yann LeCun as its first director. Meta Superintelligence Labs was created on 30 June 2025, with Alexandr Wang (former Scale AI CEO) as Meta's Chief AI Officer and Nat Friedman (former GitHub CEO) leading products and applied research. In August 2025 MSL was reorganised into four groups: TBD Lab (frontier models, led by Wang), FAIR, Products and Applied Research (Friedman) and MSL Infra (Aparna Ramani). Meta cut around 600 MSL jobs on 22 October 2025. Yann LeCun, Meta's Chief AI Scientist, left in November 2025 to found Advanced Machine Intelligence (AMI) Labs. Meta's total headcount was 78,865 at 31 December 2025 and 75,472 at 30 June 2026; in April 2026 Meta announced it would cut about 10% of staff (roughly 8,000 roles) from 20 May 2026 to offset AI investment.

## Funding, ownership and valuation
Meta is a public company controlled by Mark Zuckerberg through dual-class shares; it funds AI from operating cash flow and debt rather than venture rounds. Meta reported full-year 2025 revenue of $200.97 billion (up 22%) and net income of $60.46 billion. For Q2 2026 (reported 29 July 2026) Meta posted revenue of $60.80 billion (up 28% year on year), net income of $15.85 billion and diluted EPS of $6.18, with Q3 2026 revenue guidance of $61–64 billion. Meta's AI capital expenditure is its defining figure: 2025 capex was $72.22 billion; 2026 capex guidance was $115–135 billion in January 2026, raised to $125–145 billion in April 2026 and narrowed to $130–145 billion in July 2026. Q2 2026 capex alone was $31.08 billion. 2026 total expenses are guided at $165–169 billion. In June 2025 Meta invested about $14.3 billion for a 49% non-voting stake in Scale AI, bringing Alexandr Wang to Meta. Meta shares fell roughly 9–10% after hours following the Q2 2026 results on weaker free cash flow.

## Products and distribution
Meta distributes AI mainly through its own consumer apps. The Meta AI assistant is built into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook and Messenger, the standalone Meta AI app (launched April 2025), meta.ai, Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses and Quest headsets. Meta said Meta AI reached 1 billion monthly active users in May 2025, up from 500 million in September 2024. After Muse Spark replaced Llama as the model behind Meta AI in April 2026, Meta reported double-digit growth in sessions per user (Q1 2026) and a 60% increase in daily users of the assistant (Q2 2026), with WhatsApp the leading surface. Meta's apps reach 3.60 billion daily active people (June 2026). For developers, Meta launched the Llama API in preview in April 2025 and, in July 2026, a public-preview Meta Model API for Muse Spark 1.1 (US developers, $20 free credit). Muse models are also distributed via partner channels such as OpenRouter. Meta's infrastructure includes the Prometheus multi-gigawatt cluster in Ohio (due online in 2026), the Hyperion campus in Richland Parish, Louisiana (scalable to 5 GW), custom silicon co-developed with Broadcom, and a 1 GW El Paso, Texas data-centre venture with BlackRock announced in July 2026.

## Research, safety and openness
Meta's openness stance has shifted markedly. Zuckerberg's July 2024 essay "Open Source AI is the Path Forward" framed Llama as Meta's strategy, but by mid-2025 he said Meta would not "open source everything", citing superintelligence safety concerns. In December 2025 Bloomberg, CNBC and the New York Times reported that Meta's next frontier model (codenamed Avocado) might be closed. Muse Spark (April 2026) is proprietary, with Meta saying it "hope[s] to open-source future versions". In April 2026 Axios reported Meta would release reduced-capability open versions of future models, and on 10 August 2026 Meta released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model under the Apache 2.0 licence, distilled from Muse Spark and designed to run on a single consumer GPU (24–32 GB memory). Zuckerberg described the policy in July 2026 as "a mix of open and closed models". FAIR's historic research contributions include PyTorch, fastText, Segment Anything and self-supervised learning work; LeCun's departure in November 2025 was widely read as a shift from long-term research to product-focused frontier models. Meta's safety tooling for open models includes Llama Guard, Prompt Guard and LlamaFirewall.
